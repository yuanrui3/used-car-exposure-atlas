Where to search within three hours

Four town-to-town routes with lower typical asking prices and lower overall exposure in the destination county. On each route, both halves of the listing period showed a price advantage. A car’s time spent in the destination county still needs to be verified.

Two comparable cars on the Pacific Northwest route. In October 2026, dealers advertised two clean-title 2016 Porsche Macan S AWDs in excellent condition. The Hillsboro-area car was $12,795 with 136,274 miles. The Vancouver car was $11,599 with 134,306 miles. Its $1,196 price advantage is the starting point for the tax, fee, trip and repair comparison. In the same sample, a 2017 Toyota Camry in Vancouver was $1,200 more expensive than a same-year Camry in Washington County, Oregon, despite having 4,688 more miles. Their engine/body VIN prefixes matched, although only one ad specified the trim. These are historical ads. How the routes were selected

Each county had at least 100 supported listings. The destination scored lower under the eleven-factor index, under a seven-factor weather and environmental comparison, with each atlas category omitted in turn, and with winter weighted at 25%. The overall exposure difference exceeded the atlas’s missing-data range. Model-family bootstrap price ranges did not overlap. The asking-price signal persisted after removing the most common rounded listing location in each county. No single such location supplied half the listings. Geographic screening used county centers before checking actual road times between the named towns.

Each selected route also had at least three disjoint near-pairs with matching model family, self-reported condition, fuel, drive, transmission and seller type, at most one model year apart and within 10,000 miles. These small samples check direction only. Percentages are county-level estimates before tax, fees or repairs. Route selection is exploratory and the evidence comes from one listing source and a five-week snapshot.
