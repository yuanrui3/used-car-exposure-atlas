DATA & METHODS

What the readings mean

County boundaries follow the 2017 Census geography. Each reading retains its source period and spatial scale. A state-level value applies to the entire state.

How the annual score works

Each of the 11 categories contributes one eleventh of the score. Within a category, each selected indicator has equal weight. We rank each reading from 0 to 100 against counties with that indicator, giving tied values their shared midpoint. The final score is the average of the category scores. A value of 60 means the county averages 60 on those ranks.

Snow cover uses all twelve months, weighted by their lengths. Heat, cold and humidity use annual counts or typical-year station records. Flood and hail use average annual report days from 2015–2024. Road conditions, urbanization and airborne particles retain the source periods shown on their layers. These periods together describe a typical year.

For a missing indicator we use the national midpoint of 50 and keep its weight fixed. The range beside a county’s score shows what happens if every missing indicator takes the lowest or highest possible rank. Counties count equally in the national benchmark. Urban population share represents traffic, and state highway salt use represents treatment. City braking observations and alternative readings remain available on their own layers.

The weights are a design choice. Estimating damage, remaining life or repair costs would require vehicle histories and inspection outcomes. Download annual scores and category breakdowns.

Where a car lived

Use the locations and years in the vehicle’s ownership history. Winter residence matters for road salt. A daily commute can cross counties, and garage parking changes sun and salt-air exposure. The map describes the area around a vehicle.

How wear can be tested

Compare inspection outcomes for cars of the same model, model year, age and mileage. Use time spent in each location for corrosion and weather, and miles driven there for brakes and suspension. Reserve entire regions and later inspection years to test whether geography improves on age and mileage alone.
