# Lower-48 road exposure inputs

Run `python build_road_layers.py` with lxml. The script fetches primary-source records to `raw/` on first use. `state_road.json` uses two-digit state FIPS and includes the measured mileage behind the mapped percentages. No personal driving, maintenance or repair records enter these measures.

| Field | Denominator and interpretation | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| rough_pct | Percent of rated federal-aid road miles with IRI above 170 inches/mile or PSR below 2.6. Miles of roads, no weighting by traffic or lanes. Rates among reported miles can be unstable where reporting coverage is low; rough_coverage_pct gives the portion of total federal-aid inventory rated. | 48 states |
| unpaved_local_pct | Percent of reported local functional-class mileage with an unpaved surface, combining rural and urban local road mileage. Uses both local-road totals from table HM-51 sheets 2 and 4. Numbers are network length, not vehicle distance on those roads or dust concentration. The source cautions some states have incomplete reporting. A missing rural or urban denominator produces null. | 45 states |
| steep_pct | Share of accepted sampled road mileage with an absolute grade of at least 4.5% (HPMS classes D–F divided by A–F). Grade events are joined by route and interval to an official county reference. See the length checks below. | 40 states |

[2024 roughness table](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/statistics/2024/hm47i.cfm) · [2024 road surface table](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/statistics/2024/hm51.cfm) · [2024 roadway events](https://data.transportation.gov/d/4wpf-u82y) · [HPMS manual item 45](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/hpms/fieldmanual/page06.cfm).

## County-level data

- **Roughness** — `county_rough.json` maps five-digit FIPS to [rough percentage, measured miles, rough miles]. Source: [HPMS 2024 spatial sections](https://data.transportation.gov/d/42um-tgh5), positive IRI and section length, one-/two-way roads, functional classes 1–5. Rough means IRI >170 inches/mile. This covers 3,069 county IDs, and the section mileage differs from the state's published federal-aid summary, which adds PSR reports. Run `python build_county_rough.py`.
- **Road grades** — `county_grade.json` maps FIPS to [steep percent, accepted miles, steep miles, accepted interval count]. Of 2,372 counties with at least one interval, 1,717 have ≥10 accepted miles and ≥3 intervals. These thresholds are pilot coverage choices. State summaries require ≥100 accepted miles and ≥10 intervals. Neither result weights by vehicle miles traveled; sample panels emphasize arterials. Each state's source must contain records in A/B/C and at least one D/E/F category. Direction, longest descent, and individual vehicle travel remain unknown.
- **Road dust** — `county_dust.json` maps 2020 county FIPS to 2020 annual EPA estimated unpaved-road primary PM10 short tons, land area (square miles), and tons per land square mile. These are inventory emissions estimates, not ambient concentrations. 3,104 of 3,107 lower-48 counties reported records, and no values were imputed for Delaware's three counties. `state_dust.json` aggregates the same values and records reported land area. Run `python build_county_dust.py`. Source: [2020 EPA inventory summaries](https://www.epa.gov/air-emissions-inventories/2020-nei-supporting-data-and-summaries), [EPA road-dust methods](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-03/NEI2020_TSD_Section24_Dust_UnpavedRoads.pdf), [2020 Census Gazetteer](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/time-series/geo/gazetteer-files.2020.html).

### Rebuild grade data

Run `build_road_layers.py` once to collect the state-level grade-class records. Run `build_county_dust.py` or separately download the 2020 Census Gazetteer to `raw/2020_Gaz_counties_national.zip`. Run `build_county_rough.py` to supply the 2024 county-code inventory (accounts for newer Connecticut geography). Then run `fetch_grade_detail.py`, `build_county_grade.py`, and finally `build_road_layers.py` again.

The code downloads the original 2024 positive-valued GRADES_A … GRADES_F and COUNTY_ID events for the selected route IDs. State/route ID and begin/end mile positions must identify a single county covering the interval. The procedure rejects overlapping records, repeated class codes in an interval, intervals shorter than 0.05 mile, and any class-length sum differing from interval length by more than the greater of 0.005 mile and 2%. Some states repeat one vector of class mileages across many consecutive short pieces. The procedure reunites consecutive pieces only when they share an identical class vector and the combined extent independently matches its sum. Reconstructed vectors are counted once. The resulting intervals give a sample-based exposure indicator. Raw class sums across original published records can double-count repeated subdivisions and should never be treated as surveyed mileage.
